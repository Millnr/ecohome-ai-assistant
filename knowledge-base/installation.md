# Installation

## Overview
All EcoHome products are installed by qualified, MCS-certified (solar/battery) and OZEV-registered (EV chargers) engineers. Installation follows a structured process from initial survey to commissioning.

---

## Installation Process — Step by Step

### Step 1: Free Remote Survey (or Home Survey)
- A trained EcoHome advisor reviews your property details and energy usage
- For solar and battery: a roof assessment is carried out (satellite imagery + your photos, or an in-person visit)
- Output: a written proposal with system recommendation, indicative pricing, and expected savings

### Step 2: Detailed Quote
A formal written quote includes:
- System specification (product model, capacity, number of panels)
- All labour and materials
- Scaffolding costs (if required)
- DNO application costs (if applicable — see G98/G99 below)
- Confirmation of 0% VAT applied
- MCS certification included
- Estimated installation date

> Always obtain at least **3 quotes** from different installers before committing. Use the MCS installer finder (mcscertified.com/find-an-installer) to find accredited alternatives.

### Step 3: Surveys and Planning
- For solar systems > 3.68 kW: a **G99 application** is submitted to your DNO (Distribution Network Operator) — this can take **4–8 weeks** for approval
- Systems ≤ 3.68 kW use the simpler **G98 notification** — this is a notification, not an approval, and does not delay installation
- Planning permission: most domestic solar installs are **permitted development** and do not require planning permission. Exceptions: listed buildings, conservation areas — your surveyor will advise

### Step 4: Installation Day

**Solar panels (typical 1–2 days):**
1. Scaffolding erected (if needed) — may be a separate day
2. Mounting rails fixed to roof
3. Panels installed and wired
4. Inverter installed (usually in loft or garage)
5. AC connection to consumer unit
6. System commissioned and tested
7. Scaffolding removed
8. App set up and walkthrough with customer

**Battery storage (added to existing solar — typically 4–6 hours):**
1. Battery unit wall-mounted (garage or utility room preferred)
2. DC or AC-coupled connection to inverter
3. Gateway unit connected (if backup power option selected)
4. System commissioned and settings configured

**EV charger (typically 2–4 hours):**
1. Cable route planned from consumer unit to charger location
2. Cabling installed
3. Charger unit mounted and connected
4. Load balancing configured
5. App paired and tested

**Smart thermostat (typically 1–2 hours):**
1. Old thermostat/programmer removed
2. New thermostat wired and mounted
3. Boiler settings confirmed
4. App set up and initial schedule configured

### Step 5: Post-Installation
- MCS certificate issued (solar/battery) — allow 2–4 weeks
- DNO informed of completed installation (G98/G99 compliance)
- RECC workmanship warranty certificate issued
- Smart Export Guarantee (SEG): customer registers directly with their energy supplier using MCS certificate number

## Scaffolding
- Required for most roof installations
- Typically erected the day before panel installation and removed 1–2 days after
- Cost is included in your EcoHome quote
- Access to the front or side of the property required — inform EcoHome of any access restrictions

## G98 vs G99 — What Does This Mean for Me?

| | G98 | G99 |
|---|---|---|
| Applies to | Systems ≤ 3.68 kW | Systems > 3.68 kW |
| Process | Notification to DNO after install | Formal application to DNO before install |
| Timeline impact | None | Can add 4–8 weeks before installation can proceed |
| Who handles it? | EcoHome handles on your behalf | EcoHome handles on your behalf |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Will I need to be home during installation?**
Yes. An adult must be present throughout the installation day to provide access and sign off the completed work.

**Will my electricity be off during installation?**
There will be a short planned outage (typically 30–60 minutes) while the inverter is connected to your consumer unit. EcoHome will agree the timing with you on the day.

**How disruptive is a solar installation?**
Minimal internal disruption. Work is primarily on the roof and in the loft. The only internal work is the inverter installation (loft or garage) and the connection to the consumer unit.

**Do I need planning permission?**
Most domestic solar installations are permitted development and do not need planning permission. Listed buildings and some conservation area properties are exceptions — EcoHome will advise at survey stage.

**What happens if the weather is bad on installation day?**
If conditions are unsafe for roof work, EcoHome will reschedule. You will be contacted as early as possible.
